## Flight Operations Manager Check List

Ensure Emergency services are responding as needed for injury, fire, or other hazardous situation requiring immediate attention

Call everyone on the contact list and record who you talked to, date; time and the outcome of the conversation.

Notify necessary personnel within three hours of initial notification of incident

Keep in contact with the Flight Operations Team

Verify mishap site is located and secured

Record names, contact info, and statements from all involved recording exactly what each did and/or saw about what happened to the UAS.

Ensure wreckage is not removed or altered until released and/or all required data and information has been obtained

Gather and preserve all flight data

Provide within three hours via email all flight data, information gathered, pictures, and statements collected to GlobalSupport@edgeautonomy.io

Global Support team will initiate root cause investigation through Jira utilized data supplied by the Flight Operations Team

Tag email as “High Importance” and include “Mishap Notification” in the subject line of the email

Global Support team will complete all required notifications to company leadership

| Hadley: At 500 ft, Aircraft was commanded to RTB. We saw a couple hundered feet of climb between current position and IP1, Aircraft continued to IP2 and descended without issue while Sgt White and Myself were walking out and headed towards IP4.  It entered VTOL at PLZ and seemed to “overshoot” which it had been doing the whole time during AEWE so we expected some correction.  Approximately 100 feet AGL I issued nudge commands that Anthony relayed to the Operator-in-charge.  At approximately 50 feet, I commanded “Pause and Nudge”.  The aircraft did not pause until it was practically in the trees, approximately 25 feet AGL, it did not appear to take any nudging commands.  When it finally did pause, it shifted a couple of feet to the left and collided with the branch of the tree.  It then rolled out of the tree and impacted the ground.  As the fuel cell was still on, it immediately began to ignite the surrounding pine needles, so Sgt White and I pulled it out of the undergrowth and put out the small fire. Hansen: I was in the GCS (the Max) with the Operator while the aircraft was going to land. I was on the phone with Hadley, who was near the recovery site, since it was located about 500m away. While the aircraft was coming down in VTOL, Hadley had relayed to me to tell the Operator to nudge right. Immediately afterwards, Hadley had then stated to pause. I relayed the message to the Operator. Either the Operator did not pause immediately, or the system did not take the pause command. I reminded the Soldier that you must press and hold the button for it to pause. The aircraft did eventually pause, which I also confirmed from the looking out from the Max upon viewing the aircraft. Hadley again asked for a nudge right command. I relayed the command to the Operator. It appeared the aircraft had either gone left or forward. Hadley again stated to nudge right, which I relayed to the Operator. It appeared around that time, the aircraft had impacted a tree, about halfway up the tree. The aircraft had fell the remainder of the way and looked to be in the bushes below the tree. Upon reaching the landing spot, the aircraft was being removed from the bushy area with its center wing no longer attached. The aircraft may have landed on its side on when it fell to the bushes, the fuel cell had burned the left side of the fuselage. -Anthony SSG Hunter: During Pre Mission flight checks while on I was on the phone with my Ground Crew they reminded me to geographically sync vehicle position with launch and landing point which I did. The aircraft then began landing VTOL over the treeline which it crashed into.
