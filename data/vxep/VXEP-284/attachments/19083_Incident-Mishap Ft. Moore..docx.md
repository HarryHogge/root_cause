## Incident/Mishap Reporting Form

Details:

Name: Dana Holley, John Skogen, Scott Shilvock

Date: February 22, 2024

Location: Ft. Moore, Georgia

Description of Incident:

On Thursday the 22nd of February 2024, VXE30 battery configured aircraft 540 crashed during flight training events at Ft. Moore, Georgia. The purpose of the training was to transition the Army Ranger Regiment from their current configuration of VXE25 to their newer fleet of VXE30. The instructors teaching the course were Dana Holley, Scott Shilvock, and John Skogen.

The first two days of training were spent in the classroom teaching lessons and conducting practical exercises/simulators. The following days were spent on the flight line, conducting back seated flight events. There were 10 successful flights on Thursday prior to the crash of 540. The crash of aircraft 540 appeared to result in a total loss with damage to all components including both batteries. All flights were subsequently halted for the day, with plans to continue flying on Friday with company assets. No personnel injuries or additional damage occurred.

Personnel Involved:

John Skogen, Scott Shilvock, Dana Holley, Ranger student

Statement of Witness of John Skogen & Dana Holley:

Thursday the 22nd was our second day of flight operations at Ft. Moore training the Ranger Regiment. We successfully accomplished 10 training flights before the incident occurred. The previous flights were in fuel cell configuration, however, due to issues linking with the aircraft we decided to fly the battery configuration. While we worked/performed troubleshooting on the fuel cell aircraft, Scott Shilvock back seated a student to fly a battery configuration. When the student completed all launch wizard preflight steps, Scott notified us that the aircraft was launching.

John: At that time, I turned to watch the launch and noticed that everything looked normal.

Dana: Moments later, Scott notified me that a window had popped up on the screen. When I came to look at the screen the aircraft had already started to fly in the direction opposite of the planned mission within the VXESUAV software.

We did not close out the pop up because we hadn’t seen this error before and weren’t certain what would occur if we clicked ok. After transiting in the opposite direction, we lost sight of the aircraft. Around this time, we also clicked ok on the popup window and sometime in the following moments/minutes the aircraft crashed.

Statement of Witness of Scott Shilvock:

About 5 seconds after launching the aircraft and with it already climbing, we received a "Launch Error" dialog box that indicated we should not launch the aircraft despite it already being in the air (see image in section below).  Afraid that it may put the aircraft into a non-airborne state we hesitated to click OK as the vehicle was climbing normally.  I called Dana Holley over to take a look and the aircraft turned to a 090 heading and visually appeared to transition to normal fixed wing flight as was confirmed in the VTOL Messages section despite the flight plan to initiate the transition in a roughly 315 heading.

The aircraft transited out of sight due to the close proximity of trees blocking our visibility.  No other commands could be given until the "OK" button was pressed in the dialog box and shortly after we lost communication.  Despite taking antennas up to the roof and going mobile with another antenna in a truck that drove to the east we were unable to regain communication and the aircraft never appeared in its lost communication orbit.  Airspace controllers were notified, and we sent a team out to the last reported location.  They were able to locate the aircraft approximately 350 meters east of that last known track location.  The wing structure was roughly 100 feet up in the top of the trees with the rest of the fuselage below it on the ground.

Timeline of Events:

John and Dana started trouble shooting aircraft that had connection issues, while Scott back seated the Ranger student with the battery configured aircraft.

The student completed launch wizard preflight successfully with no errors.

During aircraft launch, pop up window indicated “LAUNCH ERROR.”

The student and instructor did not press ok on the pop-up window (due to nature of error message).

After ascent of the launch phase was complete aircraft did not fly in the direction of planned mission (planned transition: 315 degrees, actual transition: 90 degrees).

Student/instructors lost sight of aircraft (due to tree line).

Student/instructors pressed “OK” in the “LAUNCH ERROR” dialogue box.

Communications were lost with the aircraft.

Antennas were positioned (on roof & in mobile GCS) to attempt regaining communications with aircraft.

Aircraft communications were never regained.

The crashed aircraft was found 350 meters east of the last known location on VXESUAV software.
