Ther result of the Electrolytic Capacitor Saltwater Ingression and “Pop-Corn” test.

The purpose of the following test was conducted to clarify the use of seal at the top electrolytic capacitors used in wire harnesses.

Ther was a valid concern from quality control about the use of sealing materials on the top of such components. The intention of using sealing materials to prevent moist penetration between the insulator foil and the body of the capacitor.

To verify the necessity of extra seal I ran an ingression test using heavily diluted saltwater for the length of five days without any additional seal at the top of the tested capacitors.

I was measuring the Ohmic leakage across the terminals and pin to body as well.

The result of the test indicated that there was no leakage, and the test resulted in no anomaly.

The “Pop-Corn” test:

The purpose of this test was to verify if the extra layer of sealing at the top of the tested electrolytic capacitor might cause an extra increase of pressure under the condition when that capacitor over-heats and explodes.

For that test a decommissioned microwave oven was used and a 30V/10A DC Power Supply to connect the tested capacitor with reversed polarity to cause overheating and popping.

Conclusion: Ther was no difference of the magnitude of the popping between the tested (No seal, Tough Sealed, E-20) therefore the seal won’t make the impact any more severe in the case of capacitor failure.
