The detachment consisting of 2 x STALKER VXE instructors and 2x STALKER VXE pilots were prepared to fly STALKER VXE

to increase SQEP and to gain experience prior to future Regimental training plans. The above equipment was prepared and

packed the morning of the practice. This was the first flying practice of the week thus the detachment was fully rested and ready

to fly.

Airspace was allocated and opened through the DFS and flight authorisation was granted for the days flying.

The airspace allocated was Area 12 0-2000 ft AGL and the detachment set up and flew from Bustard compound in the western

end of area 12. The channel allocated was 2222 and good link quality was confirmed during preflight, prior to launch wizard

being conducted. As this was the first flight of the day and the aircraft hadn’t been flown for a period of 1 week, the detachment

conducted the required CoG check, weight test and a Magnetometer check prior to launch wizard, all of which successfully

passed and was annotated in the 705.

Launch wizard was passed successfully and the aircraft conducted a successful VTOL take off at 1048 hrs. The aircraft

transitioned and behaved as expected moving into WP1 and subsequently Orbit 2. The Aircraft commander used this as a

primary wind orbit to check local meteorological conditions prior to sending the aircraft to its further mission elements. At this

point the aircraft commander decided to change the mission profile, increasing the heights of WP 3, 4 and mission Orbit 5 to

1000ft AGL, within the airspace allocation. In addition, the pilot changed the current mission element to a ‘climb to altitude’ to

allow the UA to climb to 1000 ft AGL and transit toward the mission Orbit. Once the amendments were completed the pilot

commanded a sync, confirming with the commander that the mission was valid and this was successfully completed and sent to

the aircraft. The aircraft successfully received this command without issue and started to complete the climb and subsequently

left orbit 2 and transited at 1000 ft AGL to Orbit 5. On reaching the orbit, approximately 3.1 km from the GCS location the aircraft

commander allowed the aircraft to settle in the orbit completing several orbits. No issues with datalink were observed at this

time.

After a period of time in the main mission orbit, the commander decided to bring the height of this orbit down to 700 ft AGL. The

pilot completed the necessary correction and prepared to sync the planned changes to the UA. This was the first instance where

the GUI software became unresponsive. The command of sync was pressed, the mission was simulated successfully and

confirmed by Pilot and commander, and it attempted to sync. The ‘DOWNLOADING MISSION TO VEHICLE’ box appeared as

expected. The status bar began to increase in percentage, as expected. However after a few seconds it appeared that the

information being sent was having to cycle again. After a few more seconds, next to ‘DOWNLOADING THE MISSION TO

VEHICLE’ the message ‘(Not Responding) appeared (As seen in picture 1). The Aircraft commander attempted to cancel the

action as the datalink bar at the bottom of the GUI remained green however this was not able to be conducted. The next window

to pop up was the GCS Application is not responding window (as seen in picture 3. As the picture shows 3 options became

available. As there is no EP for this and this is the first instance this has been seen on either of the STALKER VXE GCS the

commander selected wait for the program to respond. This instantly repopulated the same pop up with the same 3 options. The

next step by the commander was to close the popup to conduct a GCS software closedown that the commander was familiar

with, in order to reacquire positive control, as has been seen in the first DASOR and utilising legacy Desert Hawk 3 ep. On

closing the pop up, the commander and pilot regained control of the software without further prompting. The ''downlink had been

lost for 15 seconds'' pop up was present and the commander acknowledged it and noticed that the UA had risen to 800 ft AGL

commanded. The commander gave the UA a positive ‘GO TO’ command to the current mission element and confirmed positive

control. Owing to this the commander and pilot agreed to gradually drag and drop the mission orbit closer to keep the aircraft

local. In the process of dragging and dropping it requires the mission to be synced to the aircraft using the same process of

amending the mission and commanding a sync. On syncing the mission to the UA the same process described above occurred

however the commander chose to close the ''GCS Software is not responding'' window as this had caused the detachment to

gain positive control in the first instance.

The pictures attached are from the second instance of the unresponsive software however what is seen is the same as the first

instance. The datalink remained green and on closing the window it became evident that the UA had lost link and the

commander confirmed positive control with a ‘GO TO’ command. After this instance it was decided by the vehicle commander to

check the IP addresses to ensure positive link, in conjunction with the software data link bar at the bottom of the Jennings

software. It was also decided that the aircraft should be allowed to complete the remaining mission profile and recover naturally

to reduce the number of inputs by the detachment. This reduces the stress/inputs to the GCS software, ensuring positive control

was retained in case of any emergency input was required. The UA completed the last 2 orbits entered the landing pattern and

recovered without any further incidents.

The aircraft and GCS were collapsed and isolated on return to camp.
