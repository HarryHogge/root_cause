# 645 operator statement.docx

Attachment 24665, docx, 15 KB, added 2024-11-22 by Pat Wells.

During the Stalker fielding team flying practice, we used SGCS 168 in conjunction with Stalker VXE 645 for the initial flight. On Monday 28th October 2024 one flight was conducted without issues. During the second day on Tuesday 29th October 2024 using the same GCS on the first flight of the day, after flying for approximately 2 Hours and 15 mins, the commander and pilot experienced what appeared to be a frozen GCS during flight, while in a mission orbit. This freeze seemingly disabled elements of the GSC Jennings software functionality. The detachment was able to maintain situational awareness as the practice was Visual Line of Sight VLOS and though the continued functionality of the System Status Display (SSD) and the UA Icon, however, were unable to give the command ‘go to’ to reset the orbit timing. On looking in the temporary FRCs there was no Emergency Procedure for a freezing GCS Software thus we took what seemed to be appropriate action to nullify the issue. This was to minimise the software and reopen it. This idea came from legacy understanding of the Jennings software from when it was utilised in Desert Hawk 3. Functionality seemed to be restored after. The Aircraft recovered without incident and the commander was in full control of the aircraft, confirming positive control by activating ‘position nudge’ during recovery. The freeze occurred again on a second flight later that day, at approximately 1 hour 40 mins into flight. The orbit had timed out and owing to how close the mission elements were, due to the VLOS restriction, the vehicle entered the landing pattern on leaving the mission orbit. The go to function is available until ip5 thus the pilot attempted to give a go to command back to the mission element. The detachment was again unable to select a go to function to allow the resetting of the mission orbit thus the UA continued in the landing pattern. Again, the UA was allowed to recover without incident. The commander initiated position nudge and had positive control as the UA recovered. At this point, the Stalker Fielding Team as a collective decided to isolate the 168 GCS laptop and use an alternative Laptop to conduct the inaugural Stalker VXE night flight. The new GCS Laptop was used with the same 645 aircraft. This flight was shorter than the previous 2 and the GCS freeze did not occur on this flight therefore it was decided to fly the second GCS from then on. On return from the night flying serial the detachment became aware that the key to the kit storage area had been lost thus the kit and equipment had to be secure in the Transit van overnight ready for use the next day. The GCS had not at this point been marked as isolated/quarantined and remained secured with all kit and equipment in the van. On 30th Oct 2024 a Stalker Fielding Team detachment deployed into area 13 to facilitate an assessment at approx 0840. The detachment set up the kit and conducted preflight. The UA and GCS passed Launch Wizard and the mission was planned and executed without incident prior to the following. As the UA was in the last few minutes of the mission orbit, the commander looked to confirm the recovery pattern and noticed the GCS software was suffering the ‘Freeze’ seen on the previous day. Although the SSD and telemetry were still functioning and the UA GUI carrot was still moving as expected, the pan zoom and go to functionalities were unavailable. The Commander and pilot minimised the Jennings software and reopened it, regaining pan and zoom functionality, however noted they were again unable to utilise the go to button on the top or the designated Ctrl G hotkey function to reroute the aircraft. The Commander and the pilot during trouble shooting thought to close the software and reboot in line with the legacy knowledge alluded to earlier however the Commander felt the safest thing was to allow the UA to recover, retaining situational awareness through the VLOS nature of the practice, telemetry, and positive control of the UA while in VTOL. The commander recovered the UA safely, taking positive control of the recovery confirming position nudge was available to ensure safety during this critical phase of flight. Post flight it was identified by the Commander that they had accidentally used the GCS which had been isolated on the 29Oct 24 rather than the secondary GCS used during the night sortie which had shown none of the freezing issues
