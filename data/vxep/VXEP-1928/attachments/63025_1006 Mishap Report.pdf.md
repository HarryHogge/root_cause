This form is to be used to report mishaps and/or issues experienced during 
Redwire customer training and demonstration events. This form is for internal 
reporting purposes only. 
 
Immediate Action Checklist  
 
❑ For a mishap involving an injury requiring medical attention, contact emergency services 
via cell phone (911). If operating on a military installation, adhere to local SOPs or pre -
accident plan for emergency services. Cooperate with emergency services as needed to 
secure the injured person(s)  
❑ Identify the mishap site (Record GPS coordinates  from last known AV location , distance 
and bearing from GCS) 
❑ Preserve the UAS wreckage and mishap site, as appropriate/if possible 
❑ Take pictures of the aircraft and mishap site  
o GCS screen shots, videos of launch and landing.  
❑ Secure and quarantine equipment that was involved in the mishap  
o This includes  the entire system such as GCS, radios, aircraft, payloads, and 
batteries 
❑ Gather and record names and contact information of all involved 
❑ Gather and record all system data (flight data recording, AAR files, mission files) 
o Refer to Post Incident Flight Data Collection  
❑ Note and record aircraft configuration and tail number (ie battery only, fuel cell, payload, 
etc) 
❑ Note and record all environmental conditions before and during the mishap  (metar, 
current weather conditions, operational conditions) 
❑ Note and record all damage to aircraft

Cal: During landing using a straight in approach, the aircraft was commanded to Land PLZ. It flew through IP1 and started descending in IP2. 
The aircraft was struggling to maintain IP2 orbit during the entire descent. While the aircraft was descending I was walking though the landing phase 
of flight and going over all the menus and controls with the student. I was monitoring the altitude and SA cam during descent. When the aircraft 
reached 200' it began descending rapidly. Wes then called out that we were extremely low. I immeditaely took the controller and commanded abort.
The aircraft began climbing and enroute back to IP1 of the landing plan. After flying back though IP1, the aircraft began descending again in IP2.
Once again, the aircraft reached 200' and immediately began losing altitude quickly. Once at roughly 100' I attempted another abort. The aircraft
began trying to climb but was unsuccessful and crashed into a field. After the crashed, we reviewed the the landing pattern and it was showing an IP2
altitude of 2' and a transition altitude of 0'. I was monitoring the student create the flight plan and am 99.9 % sure that 200' was inptu for the landing
plan. 
Wes: I saw the aircraft in IP2 Decent, everything looked normal until it started decending below what I could tell was 200ft. I called it out that it was low
and that we should abort. I mentioned that it might be a settings issue and that we should check settings out loud. Once they sent it to IP2 again the
plane started to get too low again and I said out loud to abort. The aircraft left wing tip touched the bushes but took off and started to climb. 
I called out what I was seeing and that we needed to abort/get out of landing. Even though the aircraft climbed a little it did not appear to leave landing
logic and finally dipped below a small hill and crashed.
