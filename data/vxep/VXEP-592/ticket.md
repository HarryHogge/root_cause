### Jack Waeschle, 2024-09-04

Based on my inspection of this image, the bolt does not protrude beyond the wing shift bracket. Thus far, I have found no wing bolts that are too long on any wing.
[embedded: image-20240904-231446.png]
Of the three remaining wing bolts on VXE300’s wing, none are out of the spec. The fourth was broken off to avoid collision with the non tapped portion of the wing shift bracket.
For thoroughness, see the following images/wing bolt lengths from vehicle 300.
1.85 in is the intended length for this (front left wing bolt)
[embedded: image-20240904-231749.png]

1.5 in is the intended length for each of these.
[embedded: image-20240904-231805.png]
[embedded: image-20240904-231810.png]
I have inspected the wing shift bracket for 300 and found it does not have sufficiently tapped threads. I believe people who originally discovered this problem jumped to the conclusion that the wing bolts were too long as that would explain the feeling of the wing bolts bottoming out, however I have seen no evidence that wing bolts protruded beyond the bottom of the wing shift bracket. I saw no damage that would be associated with a wing bolt forcing itself into the fuselage as it extends beyond the wing shift bracket. In practice at the field, the wing shift bracket is installed onto the vehicle and then the wing is installed onto the bracket, providing users no opportunity to see the underside of the wing shift bracket when the wing is installed. This makes it confounding whether the bolts are running into the fuselage or if the problem lies in the threaded holes themselves. 
I have installed a wing shift bracket onto a wing alone allowing inspection of the underside of of the wing shift bracket. If the screws were too long, they would clearly protrude beyond the bottom of the wing shift bracket, however with 300’s wing shift bracket this is functionally impossible as the non fully formed threads at the bottom of the holes do not allow the nylon bolts to thread further.
This can be seen clearly below as the aluminum bolt cannot be turned further in the hole.
[embedded: image-20240904-232558.png]
This lack of threads would give a user the same feedback as a long wing bolt, where it would become difficult or impossible to thread the bolt further, even though the wing was still able to move around relative to the fuselage.
