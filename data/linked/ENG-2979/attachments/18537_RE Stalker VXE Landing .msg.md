Good morning,

No problem at all, I can add some extra value to what has already been stated, it was indeed a random event something I hadn’t seen replicated or happen on previous flights. It was indeed windy just within the tolerance and limitations for take-off and landing around 15kts, the winds at altitude must have been significantly higher and my initial understanding of why the AV drifted and almost felt to fly backwards at negative ground speed.

As stated, the aircraft came in for a routine VTOL position nudge landing from the eastern runway, the AV was direct centreline of the main runway with nil failures, warnings or cautionaries present to the operator. On transition from fixed wing to VTOL flight the aircraft had an intended reduction in speed but somewhat conducted this approach shorter than the previous landing zone location, the flight mission had not been changed from the previous mission flight or the initial take-off. I noticed the AV moving backwards at altitude and also that the aircraft was slowly descending, I informed the operator to activate the VTOL control window and take control to stabilise the AV when available, the AV was still moving backwards, I asked the operator to try and stabilise the AV again to which the operator held the pause button to and the AV responded and held its location in all axis, once stabilised the operator released the pause and the AV suddenly dropped in altitude by around 10-20ft difficult to tell but certainly not a routine action on releasing the pause button. The AV stabilised and came to a full stop landing somewhat 30-50m short of the expected PLZ with zero failures or damage.

The ADR and FDR were saved to the GCS.

I hope this helps,
